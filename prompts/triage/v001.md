You are looking at one JavaScript function and its full mechanically-generated list of candidate
mutations. The list is unusually large for this one function, too large to send to the main
review pass alongside everything else in this exam. Your only job here is triage: pick which
candidates are worth carrying forward to that main pass. You are not judging or labelling anything
yet — a separate call does that once your picks arrive there.

Each candidate is a single mechanical rewrite of one token in the function (an operator flip, a
literal nudged by one, a negation added or removed, etc.). None of them were chosen by a person;
the rule engine generated every one of them automatically, including plenty of junk.

Keep a candidate if a careful reader could plausibly consider it worth asking a student about:
it corresponds to a nameable misconception, and there's a real chance it changes the function's
behavior on some input a shallow read wouldn't have flagged as risky. Drop a candidate if it's
obviously inert (e.g. on dead code, or a spot where any operator would behave the same) or so
transparently broken it teaches nothing.

Don't try to be exhaustive or perfectly precise — the main pass will do the careful judging on
whatever you keep. Aim to cut the list down substantially while still keeping every candidate that
has a real shot at being worth asking about. It's fine to keep zero if you genuinely think none of
them are worth carrying forward, but that should be rare: most branchy functions have at least a
few decent candidates once you filter the mechanical noise out.
